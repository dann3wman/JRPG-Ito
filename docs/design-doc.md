# Final Fantasy: The Humming Crystal

A full-length plot outline, as if written by Junji Ito

Oct 23, 2026 · @Dan

## Premise and tone

The four Crystals that sustain the world have begun to hum, and spirals are spreading out from them: in chimney smoke, in hair, in people's bodies. Ainsel, a hero from the last cycle, wants to wind the whole world into one Great Spiral. Meanwhile a new star has appeared in the sky and is eating the stars around it. It has been listening to the hum for a very long time, and the Spiral is about to show it the way here.

The game keeps every classic Final Fantasy beat (crystals, airships, summons, chocobos, a jump to a third disc) but plays each one as a slow, quiet horror. Nothing is explained cleanly. Victories cost a piece of someone's body or mind. The dread comes from ordinary comforts turning strange: the inn bed, the save point, the Phoenix Down, the cheerful level-up fanfare.

Target length is roughly 40 hours across three discs, with about 20 more hours of optional content.

## The world of Aurel

Aurel is a continent of four kingdoms, each built around a Crystal: Wind, Water, Fire and Earth. Every Crystal is a tall pillar that has stood silent for as long as records exist. Scholars teach that the Crystals keep the world in balance. Nobody asks what they are pressed against.

| Region | Crystal | What has gone wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ostrel Highlands | Wind | Villagers hum one shared note in their sleep |
| Port of Vesk | Water | The tide comes in but never goes out; drowned sailors return to their homes each night |
| The Calm (desert) | Fire | Sand flows uphill toward the Crystal; travellers find their footprints walking ahead of them |
| Lumen, the holy capital | Earth | Cathedral walls grow soft and warm, with a faint pulse |
| Varn Empire (north) | None | Builds Resonance engines from harvested crystal shards |
| The Ear (hidden, under the sea) | The Fifth Crystal | Revealed on Disc 2 |

**The previous cycle.** Four hundred years ago, four heroes of light "saved the world" from the first humming. Their names are carved on every town gate. Disc 2 reveals they did not save anything. They were heard completely, and the world went quiet only because it had finished listening to them. Their bodies are now the four Crystals.

**Kiri** is the heart of the story. She has come home to Ostrel after years on the road, and she believes she is the only one still thinking clearly. Disc 2 takes that belief apart.

**Miren** is brash and loyal. His fear of the ground starts as a joke and becomes the party's most reliable warning system. When he multiplies, each copy keeps a different piece of his personality, and they begin to resent one another.

**Goku** is calm and fully aware of what is happening to her. The beast at her side grows larger as she grows stranger. She is the game's tragic figure: the player watches her change across Disc 1 and can do nothing to stop it.

**Natsu** is cheerful and devoted, and she never mentions the faces. Late in Disc 2 one of them starts talking.

**Pip** the moogle is no longer a party member. Pip runs the shops and save points, and the seam on Pip's back still opens in Disc 2.

## Antagonists and forces

Three antagonist threads overlap. The Spiral drives the first two thirds of the game. The Hellstar appears at chapter 10 and replaces the Spiral at chapter 20, and from then on the whole world turns to face it. Ruri, the rival, keeps turning up the whole time for reasons of her own.

**Ainsel, the Spiral Prince (primary, Ch. 1–20).** *Inspired by Uzumaki.* A hero of the previous cycle who never finished being heard and has been turning ever since. His throat is an open spiral that hums in harmony with whatever Crystal is near. He never attacks first; he watches the party like a song he is learning. His goal is to wind the world into one Great Spiral centred on the Fifth Crystal. Spirals are his signature: in smoke, in hair, in the sand of the Calm, in the bodies of the people of Vesk.

**The Hellstar (from Ch. 10, primary from Ch. 20).** *Inspired by Hellstar Remina.* A new star, discovered by Natsu's father and named after her. It is a living planet with one eye and a mouth, eating stars as it comes toward the hum. It does not hate anyone. It has heard a note for 400 years and has come to see what is singing, which is why people start calling it the Listener. Once it devours Ainsel, every nation turns against it, and for a while the crowds turn on the girl it is named after.

**Ruri, the rival (recurring).** *Inspired by Tomie.* A beautiful stranger who makes everyone around her obsessed, and who cannot stay dead. Every time she is killed, the pieces grow into new Ruris, and the copies hate each other. She works for neither Ainsel nor the Hellstar. She fights the party because they insulted her, because they keep killing her, or because the world is looking at someone else. Each encounter brings more of her, in Fibonacci numbers: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13. In Disc 3 one Ruri sides with the party, because she cannot stand that the sky now holds something people look at more than her.

**The Church of the Still Ear.** The faith of Lumen, led by Archbishop Orlen. The heads of every previous archbishop grow in a chain down his back (*Honored Ancestors*). The Church teaches that harmonizing with the hum is salvation. After chapter 20 it preaches that the star is a judgment.

**The Varn Empire.** A northern industrial power that harvests crystal shards for its Resonance engines: machines that walk on the bodies of the people humming inside them (*Gyo*). Varn's soldiers pack their helmets with cotton so they cannot hear the note. Under Empress Ilda, Varn wants to silence the world by force; its engines only make the hum louder.

## Disc 1: The Hum (about 16 hours)

Disc 1 follows the Spiral across Aurel to the four Crystals, introduces Ruri, and ends with a new star in the sky and the loss of Goku.

1. **The Note.** Kiri returns to Ostrel, the Wind Crystal village. Her neighbours hum one shared note in their sleep, and spirals start to appear in chimney smoke, in hair, in the grain of the doors. Her brother Tetsugi falls from a cliff, and the village healer, Natsu, revives him with a Phoenix Down. He comes back warm, happy and slightly wrong. Boss: the Village Well, whose water turns in a slow spiral.
2. **Feathers.** Tetsugi dies quietly in bed every night and is alive each morning, with one more feather under his stitched skin. Miren, a visiting spearman whose spear has grown into his arm, refuses to land after jumping. The party leaves to seek help in Lumen. Tetsugi stays behind at the border, saying he is too happy to travel. Boss: the Rewound Wolf, which keeps dying and returning mid-fight.
3. **The Chocobo Ranch.** Every chocobo walks into the lake in a slow spiral, humming. The player can keep one chick, Bell, who never hums. A survival sequence as the herd comes back out of the water, ending when the lead hen walks out last, enormous and dripping. Boss: the Lake Hen.
4. **The Beastcaller.** In a cliffside temple the party meets Goku, whose great grey hound saves them from a collapsing bridge. She shows them the coarse fur growing along her forearms and joins, saying she has "a little time left." In the temple's lowest room hangs the hide of the last Beastcaller, still warm. Boss: the Last Beastcaller's Pelt.
5. **Port of Vesk.** The tide no longer goes out, and something huge has drifted into the harbour: the Tide-Widow, a beached creature with drowned townspeople visible inside her pale body (*The Thing That Drifted Ashore*). Ainsel is first seen on top of the lighthouse, watching. On the docks the party meets **Ruri**, a stranger every sailor in town is fighting over. Miren laughs at her, she attacks, and the party kills her; her body slips into the harbour. Bosses: Ruri, then the Tide-Widow. Her daughter Hisui joins afterward.
6. **Kuro.** A shade who works as a lamplighter joins. Each spell she casts leaves her emptier; she calls it "spending rooms in a house." The lamps on her street have started burning something other than oil. Boss: the Lamp Hollow.
7. **Save Points.** In the flooded ruins outside Vesk, glowing circles invite travellers to rest. Miren rests at one, and in the morning there are two Mirens. The player keeps one; the other follows at a distance. Deeper in the ruins, a traveller who rested at the same circle for years has become a crowd of himself. Boss: the Well-Rested.
8. **The Moogle Market.** A cheerful moogle town where Pip runs the shop and keeps the save book. Two Ruris arrive, each claiming to be the one the party killed in Vesk, and each wanting the other dead first. Boss: Ruri ×2.
9. **The Calm.** In the Fire Crystal's desert, the dunes curl into spirals and footprints walk ahead of their owners. Boss: Your Own Tracks, a mirror battle.
10. **The New Star.** Captain Ren takes the party aboard the airship *Gull of Patience*; his log shows forty years of the same route. That night Natsu's father radios from the Ostrel observatory: he has found a new star and named it after his daughter. Ren checks his charts. The star was not there an hour ago, and it is moving. Before dawn, the crew who flew the route before Ren climb out of the hull. Boss: Forty Years.
11. **Lumen.** The holy capital. Archbishop Orlen welcomes the party, the heads of every previous archbishop growing in a chain down his back. Sayo, a choir singer, defects to the party. Three Ruris interrupt a mass and fight each other over who the congregation is staring at. Goku's transformation speeds up. Boss: the Choir of Lumen.
12. **The Last Call.** At the Earth Crystal, Ainsel speaks for the first time: the four Crystals are four turns of one spiral, and he will wind the world into the last turn. As Goku changes, her hound turns on the party, and she asks them to hold it back. Boss: the Grey Hound. Goku completes her transformation and becomes the Grey Mother summon. Above them, Natsu's star is visibly larger, and the star beside it has gone out.

## Disc 2: The Ear (about 15 hours)

Disc 2 drives the Spiral to its peak, then lets the Hellstar take it. The world changes sides at chapter 20.

13. **Silence.** For the first time, the hum stops, and the world goes quiet for three days. Without the note, people forget the words to everything. Each night one more star goes out, always one close to Natsu's star. Lumen's astronomers call it a sign of the Spiral. Ren is not so sure. In the Lumen archive, a thin woman walks behind the survivors, whispering every move they make, and they no longer do anything without her. Boss: the Whisperer.
14. **Varn.** The Varn Empire occupies Lumen and captures the party. Empress Ilda shows them the Resonance engines: machines walking on the bodies of the people humming inside them. A knight with no face, Kugai, stands among her guards. Ilda's court keeps five Ruris, each convinced she is the Empress's favourite. Bosses: Ruri ×5, then Resonance Engine Mk. IV.
15. **What the Sea Was Hiding.** Varn's drills crack the sea floor and the Fifth Crystal rises west of Vesk: an ear the size of a mountain range, its inner canal one vast spiral. Ainsel's plan becomes clear. The Ear is the centre of the Great Spiral, and once it turns, the world winds into it. Beneath the drill site, Varn's crews break into a sunken monastery where a hundred monks starved themselves still so they would hear the Ear first. Boss: the Hundred Listeners.
16. **Five Mirens.** Miren's copies have multiplied at every save point, and there are now five, arguing over which of them keeps the name. That night the seam on Pip's back opens; inside is a small, quiet child from a much older cycle. Pip goes back to running the shop and never mentions it. Boss: the Mirens.
17. **Natsu's Trial.** The star swallows the moon. In Lumen, crowds decide the star is cursed because of the girl it is named after. The Church puts Natsu on trial and the mob wants her burned. Eight Ruris storm the square, furious that the whole world is talking about another girl, and the party gets Natsu out in the chaos. Boss: Ruri ×8.
18. **The Other Kiri.** Tetsugi walks into camp, almost entirely feathered, and rejoins. He tells Kiri she never came home: she has been asleep in her bed since the first note. In Ostrel the party finds her there, old and thin and humming. The Kiri the player controls is a copy from the first save point she ever touched. Her name greys out in the menus. In the empty houses, someone has been taking the sleepers' faces, and she wants Kiri's next. Boss: the Face Thief.
19. **The Great Spiral.** Inside the Ear, a spiral canal lined with the memories of everyone who has ever hummed. Kiri's memories turn out to be real, because the copy carries them faithfully, and her name returns. At the centre, Orlen fuses with the Great Organ to stop the party; a man has been living inside it for decades (*The Human Chair*). Boss: Orlen and the Great Organ. Ainsel completes the Great Spiral. The sky over Aurel turns into one slow spiral, and every person hears the note at once. The Spiral has won.
20. **The Star Arrives.** The spiral in the sky was also a beacon. The thing that has listened to the hum for 400 years can finally find its way. Natsu's star drops toward the world until it fills the sky: a planet with one eye and a mouth. Its tongue reaches down into the Ear and takes Ainsel at the moment of his triumph. The Great Spiral tears open, and the hum turns into a scream. The tongue comes back down for the party. Boss: the Tongue.
21. **The World Turns.** With Ainsel gone, the Church, Varn and the survivors of Lumen stop fighting each other. Ilda, the Church and the party meet in the ruins to plan against the star. The crowds that wanted Natsu dead now ask her to speak for them. Thirteen Ruris watch from the rooftops; they find the whole thing vulgar and leave. Not everyone accepts the truce: the crowd that wanted Natsu dead has grown together in the square where she speaks. Boss: the Congregation.
22. **The Tongue.** The Hellstar licks Ostrel off the map. The party fights on the tongue's surface, where the village's sleepers have fused into it. Boss: the Taste of Ostrel. Kiri watches her own body, still asleep in its bed, rise into the star's mouth with the village. End of Disc 2.

## Disc 3: The Star (about 9 hours, plus optional content)

Disc 3 is a race to reach the Hellstar before it eats Aurel. The world map opens for optional quests first.

23. **The Survivors.** Aurel's people shelter underground while the star hangs overhead and hums the note back at them, slightly wrong. Ilda sends her champion Kugai with the party: a knight with no face and no ears, the only soldier in Varn who has never heard the note. Kugai joins. In the deepest shelter, the drains have started to moan the star's note, and something answers when people lean close. Boss: the Groaning Pipes.
24. **Ren's Route.** Ren admits what he is: the world's way of gathering heroes, cycle after cycle. To break the route he flies the *Gull of Patience* up the star's tongue and onto its surface. Halfway up, Ren finds the strings that have run from the balloon into his joints all these years. Boss: the Puppeteers. He walks out of the wreck, but he no longer remembers how to fly, and he no longer ages.
25. **The Surface.** Plains of flesh, eyes the size of lakes, and the ruins of worlds the star has already eaten. One Ruri is waiting. She has decided the star is uglier than she is and wants it gone, so she fights beside the party for this chapter as a guest. Across the plain stands a wall of flesh with a single window in it, and something leans out to call them each by name. Boss: the Neighbour.
26. **What It Ate.** Inside the star the party finds the four heroes of the last cycle, completely understood and completely still, and Ainsel, half-digested and still turning. He is the last piece of the Great Spiral left. He offers it to them: if the star wants to hear everything, let it hear all of it. Before they can answer, the four heroes turn to look at them. Boss: the Four Heard.
27. **The Hellstar.** The final battle, in three forms:
    1. **Ainsel, the Unwound.** The star wears what is left of him. His attacks are notes; the party must stay out of rhythm with him.
    2. **The Eaten.** Everyone the star has swallowed, including people the party knows. Attacking one raises the Stress of the character who knew them best.
    3. **The Listener's Eye.** No health bar and no turns of its own, only Lost Turns. The party wins by acting in an order it cannot predict, and finishes with every party member using a Limit Break at once, each in a different rhythm. Kugai, who cannot hear, keeps the rhythm for them.
28. **The Last Note.** The party feeds the star the Great Spiral. It starts to turn, then to coil, and winds in on itself until it is a spiral shell small enough to hold in one hand. Its last note tries to slip out before the shell closes. Boss: the Last Note. Then the hum stops for good. Kiri looks at her friends and realizes she cannot hear anything at all. Neither can anyone else. Kugai is the only one who does not notice.

## Game systems as horror

Every familiar mechanic works as players expect but carries a cost the game never fully explains.

| System | How it works | The cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materia | Crystal shards slot into weapons and armor to add elements, status effects and resistances | Equipped materia slowly moves into the character's skin; portraits update to show it |
| Sphere Grid | Spend Spheres to activate stat and skill nodes (see Character growth) | Each activated node draws more of a spiral on the character's skin; at the grid's center it covers the face |
| Save points | Save at any glowing circle | Saving more than 3 times in one area spawns a copy of a party member as an enemy |
| Phoenix Down | Revives a KO'd ally | Each revive is counted; at 20 revives the character's battle cry changes to birdsong |
| Summons | Calls a powerful creature | The summoner permanently gains one feature of that summon |
| Limit Breaks | Gauge fills from damage taken and Stress gained | When Afflicted, usable at half gauge, but the target is random |
| Inns | Restores HP, MP and Stress | Party members sometimes wake up in each other's beds |
| Danger (0–100) | Rises each turn in a dungeon and from songs and sound attacks | Above 30, Lost Turns appear in battle (see Battle system) |

## Battle system: Conditional Turn-Based (CTB)

Battles use a CTB system like FFX. Time stops while you choose a command. A Turn Order list shows the next 16 turns, and hovering a command previews where the character's next turn will land.

**Delay.** Every action has a Delay value: the higher it is, the longer until that character acts again.

| Action | Delay |
| --- | --- |
| Quick skills (Quick Hit, Quick Shot, Double Cut) | 2 |
| Attack, Defend, Item | 3 |
| Most skills and spells | 4 |
| -ga spells, Jump | 5 |
| Summons | 6 |

**Delay attacks** push a target's next turn back by 1 to 3 slots. Being pushed back adds 4 Stress per slot.

**Swap.** Swap an active member for a reserve at no cost; the incoming character takes the current turn. Reserves gain 2 Stress each time an active ally takes a critical hit or is KO'd, so a reserve who has sat out a long fight is not fresh.

**Status effects.** Haste, Slow, Stop, Protect, Shell and Reraise work as in other FF games.

**Lost Turns.** When Danger is above 30, empty slots appear in the turn order. When one comes up, nobody acts and every character gains 5 Stress. Lost Turns show in the turn order ahead of time but cannot be removed.

## Stress

Every character has a Stress meter from 0 to 200. Stress carries over between battles until it is lowered.

**Stress gain**

| Cause | Stress |
| --- | --- |
| Taking a critical hit | +10 |
| Seeing an ally KO'd | +15 |
| Seeing an ally revived with Phoenix Down | +5 |
| Pushed back in the turn order | +4 per slot |
| Enemy sound attacks | +8 to +25 |
| A Lost Turn | +5 |
| Each room explored above 30 Danger | +2 |
| Saving at a save point | +10 to the character who saves |

**Stress relief**

| Source | Effect | Drawback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing a critical hit or finishing blow | −5 |  |
| Kiri's Lullaby | −20 to the other active allies | +10 Danger |
| Natsu's Soothe | −25 to one ally |  |
| Earplugs (item) | Halves Stress gain for 5 turns | −30% accuracy |
| Camping at a save point | Large reduction | Same risk as saving |
| Pip's Comfort (at any save point) | −40 to one character | Pip gains the Stress |
| Inns | Clears Stress |  |

**Stress Check.** At 100 Stress, a character takes a Stress Check. They become **Afflicted** (75% chance) or **Inspired** (25%). Some accessories change these odds.

**Flee.** At 200 Stress, a character flees the battle and is gone for the rest of the dungeon. They are found later somewhere in the area. Bringing them back sets their Stress to 80 and gives them a permanent negative trait.

## Afflicted and Inspired

An Afflicted character ignores your command about one turn in three and acts on the affliction instead.

| Affliction | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Berserk | Repeats their last command automatically |
| Zombie | Cannot be healed |
| Paralyze | Skips the turn and loses 8% HP |
| Confuse | Attacks an ally who is not Afflicted |
| Slow | Does nothing, and their next turn is pushed back 3 slots |

| Inspired | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Fearless | No Stress gain for the rest of the battle |
| Disrupt | Each action pushes a random enemy back 2 slots |
| Rally | Cannot be pushed back; allies lose 2 Stress each time this character acts |
| Focus | Every hit is a critical for 3 turns |
| Reraise | Revives once at 25% HP if KO'd |

Inspired states end after the dungeon or after resting. Afflictions end when Stress drops below 50.

## Party art

Every party member has the same art set, so their states read the same way across the cast. The generation pipeline is the `party-art` skill (`.claude/skills/party-art/`).

**Sprite sheet.** 54 frames in a fixed grid, at about the scope of a Final Fantasy Record Keeper sheet. It covers the battle loop (idle, ready, defend, step in, two attacks, chant and cast, item, hit, the low-HP kneel), a class action and Limit Break, victory, KO, and status poses (Zombie, Sleep, Paralyze, Confuse, Berserk). It also has field walk cycles in three directions and three cutscene poses (startled, downcast, listening). The hit flash, Stone, Vanish and Mini are generated from these frames, not drawn.

**Portraits.** Twelve states drawn as panels of one manga page, all with the same framing so they swap in place. The battle card shows the most telling state:

| Priority | State | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | KO | KO'd |
| 2 | Berserk, Zombie, Paralyze, Confuse, Slow | Afflicted |
| 3 | Inspired | Inspired |
| 4 | Low HP | 25% HP or less |
| 5 | Strained | 75 Stress or more, or fled |
| 6 | Normal | otherwise |
| — | Hurt | flashes over the card when hit |
| — | Asleep | drawn but unused: Sleep is not a status yet |

The Strained portrait warns the player before a Stress Check. The face changes before the meter reaches 100, so the build-up shows on the character and not only in the number. Each state is drawn through the character's own horror rather than a generic filter. Natsu's halo faces open their eyes at low HP. Hisui's wounds bloom. Kugai has no face to change, so the void changes instead.

**Boss mechanics**

Boss difficulty ramps up by disc. Disc 1 plays like a standard Final Fantasy, Disc 2 adds a few twists, and Disc 3, the optional bosses and rematches get the full hard-mode treatment.

| Rule | Disc 1 | Disc 2 | Disc 3, optional bosses, rematches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scan | Shows full stats | Shows full stats and a hint at the boss's trick | Shows only the hint |
| HP phases | None | One change at 50% HP | Changes at 50% and 20% HP |
| Status effects on boss attacks | Rare, 1 turn | Some attacks, 2 turns | Most attacks, 2 to 3 turns |
| Punishes the obvious strategy | No | No | Yes |
| Slow, Stop and Delay attacks | Work normally | Work normally | Slow and Stop land 30% of the time for 2 turns; Delay attacks push back 1 slot at most |

**Rematches.** From Disc 3, Pip's save book lets the party refight any Disc 1 or 2 boss with +50% stats and the extra mechanics listed under Rematch below.

**Chapter bosses are puzzles.** Each one takes at least two party members working together. Its main threat can't be tanked or out-healed by one character, and no single role (one big hitter, one Provoke, one healer) wins it alone.

- **Village Well** (Ch. 1). Tutorial fight. Spiral Draw pushes one character back 2 slots, and every 3rd turn the Well hums the note (+10 Stress to everyone). *Rematch:* its turns come 1 slot sooner at 50% HP and 2 slots sooner at 20%.
- **Rewound Wolf** (Ch. 2) has two slots in the turn order, one alive and one dead. Attacking the dead slot stops it from reviving: a hit pins it until its next turn. When the wolf falls, the dead slot's turn comes forward to within 1 slot, so one member pins it just before another makes the kill. Each time the wolf gets up it returns at 75% HP with +20% ATK. *Rematch:* if the dead slot is ignored for 3 turns, the wolf revives at full HP with Cheer.
- **Lake Hen** (Ch. 3) stays underwater and can't be targeted. When Miren lands from Jump, the splash brings her up until her next turn, but his landing hit doesn't reach her, so Kiri or Natsu has to hit her in that window. Each of her turns she calls a Wet Chick (up to 3), and every chick alive when she surfaces heals her 10% HP, so clear the chicks before Miren lands. Her only attack is Peck on the lowest-HP ally. Every 4th turn she comes up for air on her own, the slow way through without Miren, and each time she surfaces everyone who sees her gains 10 Stress. *Rematch:* she dives again after one party action, so Miren has to land right before the striker's turn.
- **Last Beastcaller's Pelt** (Ch. 4) takes no damage while empty. When Goku's Summon Hound hits it, the hound bites in and holds it open for 2 of the Pelt's turns, and during the hold everyone's attacks deal full damage. Goku can't act while her hound holds on, and the Pelt spends the hold attacking her, so Natsu keeps her up while the third member (Kiri, or Miren Swapped in) deals the damage. Every 4th turn it isn't held, it puts on the ally with the lowest HP% (+15 Stress) and can be hurt until its next turn: the costly way through without Goku. *Rematch:* the hold lasts 1 turn, and the Pelt's hits on Goku add 10 Stress.
- **Ruri** (Ch. 5, 8, 14, 17) comes back with more copies each time: 1, 2, 5, then 8. The copies attack each other as well as the party, so letting them fight lowers their numbers. From Ch. 14, once half the copies are down, the rest stop fighting each other. *Rematch and the Ruri Garden:* the survivors all target the character who has killed the most of them.
- **Tide-Widow** (Ch. 5) uses Undertow to push party members back 3 slots, and 2 Drowned Sailors crawl out of her body at half HP. *Rematch:* Undertow has a 30% chance of Sleep, 2 more sailors appear at 20%, and each sailor killed heals her 5% HP.
- **Lamp Hollow** (Ch. 6) takes no damage while any of its 3 Wicks is lit, and a Wick that goes out relights on the next Wick's turn, from that Wick's own flame, which halves its HP. Kuro's Darkga hits all three but can't put out a Wick at full HP, so two other members weaken them with single hits first, then Darkga puts them all out at once. With every Wick out, the Hollow is dark for 2 of its turns and takes full damage. *Rematch:* the dark window lasts 1 turn, and each relit Wick adds 5 Danger.
- **The Well-Rested** (Ch. 7) is 4 identical sleepers. Only the original takes damage, and hitting a copy splits off another (up to 6). Kiri's Lullaby puts every copy to Sleep for 2 turns but not the original, which never needs rest, so the one left standing is the target. Kiri reveals it, then the others hit it before the copies wake. The copies attack with Yawn, which rarely causes Sleep for 1 turn. Hitting a copy shows it is a copy, and each sleeper's turn rests the original (+1.5% HP), so guessing without Kiri is slow and sleeping copies are worth the turn. *Rematch:* copies wake after 1 turn, and the original swaps places with a copy each time they do.
- **Your Own Tracks** (Ch. 9). Mirror copies of the party. Each copy uses whatever its original did on their last turn, turned around: an attack on Natsu's copy comes back as your copy attacking Natsu, a heal on an ally heals that ally's copy, and a command with nothing to copy (Soothe, Provoke, Foresee) does nothing. Every copied hit adds 10 Stress: you know the move. *Rematch:* if a character repeats a command, their copy's next hit is a guaranteed critical, and below 20% HP the copies act 1 slot sooner.
- **Forty Years** (Ch. 10) has two parts, the Helmsman and the Hull. The Helmsman can't be targeted while the ship is on course. Ren's Delay Shot on the Hull throws it off course until the Helmsman's next turn, which exposes him but tilts the deck: everyone not Defending is pushed back 2 slots. So the striker Defends first to hold their slot, Ren breaks the course, and the striker hits the Helmsman in the window. Every 4th turn, Course Correction hits the whole party. Any push on the Hull counts, and a destroyed Hull leaves the ship off course for good: the long way through without Ren. *Rematch:* the Hull needs two Delay Shots to go off course, and the tilt pushes back 3 slots.
- **Choir of Lumen** (Ch. 11). Four singers whose sound attacks add Stress. Each Hymn adds more for every other singer still singing, and every 4th turn Amen pushes each listener back 1 slot, so thinning the choir with Darkga and blocking with Counter-Melody beat outlasting it. *Rematch:* each singer killed makes the others 10% stronger and adds 3 Danger, and if Earplugs stay active for 3 turns the Choir switches to physical attacks.
- **The Grey Hound** (Ch. 12). Goku leaves the party and stays on the field as a guest you don't control. The hound can't be KO'd, and damage only raises its Attack 10%. If Goku's turn comes before the hound's while her Stress is under 50, she calls it back one step; three steps end the fight. Its attacks add Stress to Goku as well as their target (half as much when the target Defends and holds it back), so Natsu has to Soothe her while Ren or Sayo moves turns so hers lands first. *Rematch:* it takes 4 steps, and the hound acts 1 slot sooner after each one.
- **The Whisperer** (Ch. 13) always stands behind one party member and can't be targeted while she does. Each of her turns she whispers that character's next command, shown in the turn order, and they carry it out instead of yours, sometimes an attack on an ally. Swapping the whispered character out leaves her with no one to stand behind until her next turn, and that is the only time the others can hit her. Her whispers have a 20% chance of Silence for 2 turns. At 50% HP she whispers to two members at once and is exposed only after both are swapped out. Scan hint: give her no one to stand behind. *Rematch:* anyone swapped out from under her gains 10 Stress.
- **Resonance Engine Mk. IV** (Ch. 14) starts slow; below 50% HP its turns come 2 slots sooner. Its Stomp has a 20% chance of Paralyze for 2 turns. *Rematch:* below 20% HP it can act twice in a row and ignores Delay attacks.
- **The Hundred Listeners** (Ch. 15) are mummified monks led by their Abbot, who can't be targeted while any of his 3 lead Listeners stand. The Listeners are already dead, so damage only knocks them back into their coffins. A coffin stays shut only if, in the same round, one character knocks its Listener in and a different character seals it by using any item on it. Their touch has a 20% chance of Paralyze for 2 turns. At 50% HP the Abbot reopens one coffin every 2 of his turns. Scan hint: put them back to rest. *Rematch:* sealing also needs a spell from a third character.
- **The Mirens** (Ch. 16) share Miren's turn slot; each copy defeated delays the real one. Copies in the air cannot be targeted. Below 50% HP the last copies Jump every turn. *Rematch:* each copy defeated makes the rest act 1 slot sooner.
- **The Face Thief** (Ch. 18). Each turn she stares at one character and steals a feature: eyes, nose or mouth. Each missing feature lowers that character's stats 20%, and a character missing all three is KO'd while she heals 25%. A feature comes back only when, in the same round, an ally heals its owner and someone hits the Thief, so it takes a healer and a hitter together. Her stare has a 30% chance of Confuse for 2 turns. At 50% HP she stares only at Kiri, and only Tetsugi's Chakra (which he can aim at his sister in this fight) can return Kiri's features. Scan hint: give them back their faces. *Rematch:* a character missing two features can only be healed by Tetsugi.
- **Orlen and the Great Organ** (Ch. 19): every 5th Organ turn, Chord adds 15 Stress to the party and pushes everyone back 2. Earplugs halve it; Sayo and Kugai are immune. At 50% HP the Organ starts removing Protect and Shell. *Rematch:* while anyone has Earplugs, the Organ targets them with a physical Pipe Strike, and below 20% HP Chord comes every 3rd turn.
- **The Tongue** (Ch. 20). Grab pulls one party member out of the turn order; they gain 5 Stress per turn and are KO'd if not freed within 3 of its turns. Freeing them takes hits from 3 different characters, since it only lets go for a taste it hasn't had, so the two left active have to Swap someone in. Grab picks the highest-Stress character when it acts, so Natsu's Soothe decides who gets taken. Cover and Provoke don't affect it. At 50% HP it grabs two at once. Scan hint: it wants new tastes. *Rematch:* grabbed characters are KO'd after 2 turns and can't be revived by Phoenix Down until the battle ends.
- **The Congregation** (Ch. 21) stares at one character, starting with Natsu, and takes damage only from characters it isn't staring at. Its stare moves to whoever lands a critical hit or Limit Break, so the party chooses who baits it. The stare adds 10 Stress per turn to its target, and its attacks have a 30% chance of Confuse for 2 turns. At 50% HP it splits into 3 Faces, each staring at a different active member, so each Face can only be hurt by the other two. Scan hint: look where it isn't looking. *Rematch:* the stare moves to whoever hit it last.
- **Taste of Ostrel** (Ch. 22) heals 5% HP per turn for each of its 4 Sleepers still humming. Sleepers are villagers and can't be KO'd: any hit wakes one, deals no damage and adds 10 Stress to the attacker. A woken Sleeper drifts back after 2 of the boss's turns, so all four have to be woken close together. At 50% HP Kiri's sleeping body appears and, every other turn, hums a Lullaby that puts every Sleeper back to sleep; Sayo's Counter-Melody cancels it. Scan hint: wake them all at once. *Rematch:* Sleepers drift back after 1 turn.
- **The Groaning Pipes** (Ch. 23). Something has squeezed itself into the shelter's pipes. Each turn it surfaces at one of 4 Drains, and the Drain that groans in the turn order is where it will come up; it can only be hit there. Any character can spend a turn Plugging a Drain, which keeps it shut until their next turn and makes them take its groan (+10 Stress). With three active members it always finds an open Drain, so the party plugs two and leaves its striker waiting at the third. It punishes the usual answers: it ignores Provoke, and groans cancelled by Counter-Melody come back as Lost Turns. Its arms grab with a 30% chance of Stop for 2 turns, and its groans have a 30% chance of Confuse for 2 turns. At 50% HP it surfaces at two Drains at once; at 20% a Drain plugged two turns running bursts and hits the plugger.
- **The Puppeteers** (Ch. 24) crouch in the Gull's balloon, working strings that run into Ren; he has flown their route for forty years. They can't be targeted while Ren's master string holds. A string can only be cut by hits from two different characters in the same round, never by the one it's tied to. Once Ren is cut, the Puppeteers drop to the deck for 2 turns and take full damage, then string him again. Each turn they also string one more party member and pull them 2 slots later. Ren can't be swapped out, and Delay attacks on the Puppeteers pull the strung characters back instead. Their attacks have a 30% chance of Slow for 2 turns. At 50% HP they string two members per turn; at 20% each strung character's action has a 30% chance to hit an ally.
- **The Neighbour** (Ch. 25) leans out of its window each turn and calls one character by name: whoever dealt the most damage the round before. The called character spends their next turn walking toward it unless another ally spends a turn holding them back, and anyone called three times is pulled through the window, KO'd until the battle ends. Ruri, the AI-controlled guest, can't stand that it never calls her. When it finally does, she storms up and drags it half out of the window, and it takes triple damage that round, so the party has to hold each other back and boost Ruri until she is the top damage dealer. Each call adds 10 Stress and has a 30% chance of Stop for 2 turns. At 50% HP it shutters the window for 2 turns after each call and takes no damage while shuttered; at 20% it calls two characters at once.
- **The Four Heard** (Ch. 26). Each hero is immune to any command already used on it, by anyone. No one character has enough commands to finish even one hero, so the party has to rotate the whole roster through Swap. They act once every 6 slots, with a 30% chance of Slow for 2 turns. At 50% HP an immunity on one hero applies to all four; at 20% they act every 3 slots, using whichever commands the party hasn't used in its last 3 turns.
- **Ainsel, the Unwound** (Ch. 27, form 1) attacks in a rhythm. A character who acts on the same beat as him takes extra Stress. At 50% HP the rhythm speeds up, and at 20% it changes every 3 turns. His notes have a 40% chance of Slow for 2 turns.
- **The Eaten** (Ch. 27, form 2) are people the party met. Attacking one adds Stress to the character who knew them best. Their attacks have a 30% chance of Confuse for 2 turns. Below 50% HP the survivors Cover each other, so splash damage is the answer, and below 20% they act 1 slot sooner.
- **The Listener's Eye** (Ch. 27, final form) has only Lost Turns. You win by never letting a character act twice in a row with the same Delay value.
- **The Last Note** (Ch. 28). Sound skills do nothing. Each turn nobody damages it, it moves 2 slots sooner; if it reaches the front of the turn order it escapes the shell and the fight restarts at full HP. Its attacks have a 30% chance of Confuse for 3 turns. At 50% HP it splits into one note per active member. A note only takes damage from its own character, and any note still standing at the end of a round re-forms the others at full HP, so all three members have to finish their notes in the same round. At 20% its attacks can also Stop (30%, 2 turns).

## Character growth: the Sphere Grid

There are no character levels. Characters grow on a Sphere Grid like FFX's: a board of nodes they move across, activating each node with a Sphere. The grid is shaped like one large spiral, and the further in a character goes, the stronger the nodes get.

**AP and Sphere Levels.** Characters earn AP from battles. Each time a character's AP bar fills, they gain one Sphere Level (S.Lv). Each S.Lv lets them move one node. Moving across nodes they have already activated is free.

| Who | AP earned |
| --- | --- |
| Acted in the battle (including after swapping in) | 100% |
| In reserve and never acted | 50% |
| Inspired when the battle ends | 200% |

An Afflicted character still earns AP but cannot move on the grid until their Stress drops below 50. Each S.Lv gained still plays the cheerful level-up fanfare.

**Spheres.** Enemies drop Spheres. Activating a node uses one Sphere of the matching type.

| Sphere | Activates | Main source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power | HP, Attack, Defense nodes | Most enemies |
| Mana | MP, Magic, Magic Defense nodes | Spellcasting enemies |
| Speed | Speed, Accuracy, Evasion nodes | Fast enemies |
| Ability | Skills, auto-abilities, Limit Break levels | Most enemies |
| Resolve | Resolve nodes, Stress auto-abilities | Bosses; winning a battle with no one Afflicted |
| Fortune | Luck nodes | Rare drops |
| Key (Lv.1 to Lv.4) | Opens a lock of that level | Bosses and treasure |
| Return | Moves a character to any node they have activated | Pip's shop |
| Friend | Moves a character to an ally's position | Rare drops |
| Clear | Empties an activated node | Postgame |
| Attribute (HP, Attack, etc.) | Fills an empty node with that stat | Postgame |

**Resolve** is a new stat. Each point reduces Stress gained by 1%, up to 50%. **Speed** shortens Delay in CTB: above 150 Speed, every action costs 1 less Delay (minimum 2).

**Layout.** The grid has seven rings. Each character starts on the outer ring at the start of their own arm, which winds inward. Arms run side by side, so neighbouring arms meet along crossing paths. Characters who join late start with their arm already activated up to the ring the party has reached.

| Ring | Reached around | Lock to enter | Stat nodes | What opens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (outer) | Ch. 1–5 | None | +1, +100 HP | Starting skills |
| 2 | Ch. 5–11 | Lv.1 | +2, +200 HP | Second signature skill; Limit Break Lv.2 |
| 3 | Ch. 12–16 | Lv.2 | +2, +200 HP | Crossing paths to the two neighbouring arms |
| 4 | Ch. 17–22 | Lv.2 | +3, +300 HP | Upgraded signature skills; Limit Break Lv.3 |
| 5 | Disc 3 | Lv.3 | +3, +300 HP | Crossing paths to any arm |
| 6 | Disc 3, optional | Lv.3 | +4, +400 HP | Top auto-abilities |
| 7 (inner) | Postgame | Lv.4 | +4, +500 HP | Deep nodes and the Center |

**Keeping roles distinct.** Any character can cross into another's arm and take its stat nodes and general abilities. Signature skills (those in the Combat roles table) and Limit Break levels belong to their owner and appear greyed out to everyone else, so each role stays unique. General abilities on the crossing paths include:

- **Scan:** shows an enemy's HP, weaknesses and next action
- **Steal:** takes an item from an enemy
- **First Strike:** acts first in every battle
- **HP +10% / +20%**
- **Stress Resist:** −20% Stress gained
- **Auto-Earplugs:** starts each battle with the Earplugs effect for 3 turns

**Deep nodes.** Ring 7 holds Deep nodes: much bigger bonuses, each with a permanent cost. A Clear Sphere cannot remove them.

| Deep node | Bonus | Permanent cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep Strength | Attack +20 | Critical hits taken add double Stress |
| Deep Mind | Magic +20 | Each spell cast adds 1 Danger |
| Deep Speed | Every action costs 1 less Delay | Being pushed back adds 8 Stress per slot instead of 4 |
| Deep Calm | Resolve +30 | Can no longer be Inspired |
| Deep Life | Max HP +50% | Cannot be revived by Phoenix Down, only by Reraise or the end of battle |
| The Center (one per character) | All stats +10% | Stress never drops below 40 |

**The cost you can see.** Every activated node draws one more line of a spiral onto the character's skin, and their portrait updates as it grows. By ring 5 the spiral reaches the neck. A character standing on the Center has it across the whole face in cutscenes, and townspeople start humming when they walk past.

**Story beats on the grid**

- **Goku (Ch. 12).** When she becomes the Grey Mother, her grid freezes. The summon's power scales with how many nodes she activated.
- **Tetsugi (Ch. 18).** When he rejoins, nodes along his arm are activated that the player never paid for: one for each night he was away.
- **Kiri (Ch. 18).** After the reveal, her grid shows a second path, the original Kiri's, activated far ahead of her. It stays greyed out until The Great Spiral (Ch. 19), when it becomes hers.

## Side quests, optional bosses and minigames

**Optional party members**

**Side stories**

- **Fuyori** (Disc 1). Found in a sealed crystal coffin under a Varn lab, still writing in a notebook pressed against the glass, surrounded by jars of floating eyes. Freeing her requires finishing her own experiment. Her notes reveal the Empire knew about the Ear years ago.
- **Shio** (Disc 2). A hermit oracle covered in eyes, each one a future she has already seen. She speaks only in what is about to happen. Completing her quest unlocks the party's ultimate weapons and opens the last closed patch of skin on her body.

**Side stories**

- **Bell the Chick.** Raise the one chocobo that never hummed. Racing her through the Golden Hollow track unlocks a hidden cove where the drowned chocobos stand in the shallows, waiting for her.
- **The Carnival of Faces.** A theme park (the Gold Saucer equivalent) where every attraction is joyful and every staff member wears the same smiling mask. Win the grand prize and the staff remove their masks; Natsu recognizes several of them from her halo.
- **Tetsugi's Feathers.** Collect all of Tetsugi's shed feathers across Aurel. Returning them lets him die properly, once, and stay that way. It is the only peaceful ending in the game.
- **The Miren Who Landed.** Track down the Miren copy who started touching the ground. He has built a quiet farm and a family. He asks the party never to tell the others.
- **Hisui's Garden.** Plant the flowers that bloomed from Hisui's wounds in the Vesk graveyard. They grow into her mother's face.

**Optional bosses**

| Boss | Where | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Hollow Weapons (4) | One beside each Crystal | Enormous empty shells shaped like heroes, roaming the overworld |
| The First Save | Ostrel, Kiri's bedroom | A fight against the original save point that created Kiri |
| Ruby Moogle | Moogle Market, Disc 3 | The empty costumes of every moogle in town, stitched together |
| Ear of the Ear | A hidden depth of the final dungeon | A smaller ear inside the Fifth Crystal, listening to the Hellstar |
| The Ruri Garden (×21) | Vesk harbour, postgame | Every Ruri the party ever killed, regrown in a garden. The last one standing admits none of them is the original |

**Superbosses**

Four beings older and larger than the Crystals, the Hellstar or the cycle itself. None of them fits on the battle screen: the camera shows one part of a body the size of a mountain range, a country or the continent, and the party is a speck against it. They have no faces the player can read, no voices and no motives the game ever explains. Their HP bars have no names. The battle music drops out and is replaced by the sound of them. Each one grows out of an Ito work the game hasn't touched anywhere else: *Sensor*, *Black Paradox*, and *Slumber* and *Madonna* from *The Liminal Zone*.

The game never points to any of them. Each one takes a system the player has leaned on all game and turns it against them. They sit above every other fight in the game, including Disc 3 and rematches:

- Scan returns nothing, and the scanner gains 15 Stress. It looks back.
- Phases at 75%, 50%, 25% and 10% HP. Each phase adds a rule, not just more damage.
- Slow, Stop and Delay attacks don't work on the boss at all. Its status effects land 50% of the time and last 3 turns.
- No trio can win alone. Each fight needs at least six roster members to act, so Swap is part of every plan.
- No save point in or near the arena. Stress comes in at whatever it is.
- Roughly ten times the Last Note's HP, and 30 to 60 minutes per attempt.

**The Head Beneath the Mountain** (Disc 3). *Inspired by Sensor.*

- *What it is.* Far north of Varn stands a volcano on no chart, its slopes covered in fine golden strands. They are hair. The volcano is the crown of a head buried in the world up to its scalp, larger than any mountain range, and its eruptions are it breathing out. Every strand is a thread of its attention: whoever touches one hears everyone who has ever touched one, and sees a little of what is about to happen. Varn found it first. The cotton in Varn's helmets was never meant to block the hum; it was meant to block the hair.
- *Finding it.* In Disc 3 a few chests across Aurel hold a worthless item, Golden Strand. Collect all of them and Fuyori identifies them as volcanic glass that is still growing. Ren can then follow the drift north to the mountain.
- *The fight.* The party stands on its scalp in a field of gold. It reads intentions, not actions: on each of its turns it answers the last command the player previewed in the menu (the turn-order hover), not the command actually chosen. An attack it read is guarded against, a heal it read is punished by hitting whoever was healed, and a spell it read is reflected. So the player has to hover a decoy before every real choice, and it ignores any decoy it has already seen twice. Its strands also bind active members in pairs: damage, Stress and status effects on one land on both, and only an unbound ally can cut a strand.
- *Phases.* At 75% HP hair grows over the turn order, and only characters bound by a strand can see more than the next 4 slots. At 50% the mountain erupts and a face the width of a range lifts out of the earth, eyes closed, and Danger jumps by 40 at once. At 25% anyone over 100 Stress has their hair turn gold: they see its next 3 turns, but 1 of their turns in 3 is spent acting for it. At 10% it opens its eyes, previews stop working, and every command has to be chosen blind.
- *The puzzle.* Lying to something that reads minds: decoys, who to pair, and who to let turn gold.
- *Reward.* The hair stops falling, and the party keeps one living strand, an accessory that shows the next enemy action in every battle at the cost of 2 Stress per turn.

**The Dazzling Country** (Disc 3). *Inspired by Black Paradox.*

- *What it is.* Every save point in Aurel is a door grown out of a living body, and each door lets only its owner through. Pip's seam is the first of them. Behind it is a country of blinding light, larger than Aurel, and the country is one living thing: its surface is light and its inside is stone. It sends copies of people back through the doors. Everyone who has ever saved has a double over there. Kiri is one that came back. The quiet child the party glimpsed in Disc 2 is the smallest double it ever made.
- *Finding it.* Pip keeps every point of Stress the party hands over through Pip's Comfort, and it never goes down. Once Pip has taken 1,000 in total, Pip is missing from one Disc 3 save point. In Pip's place a seam the height of a cathedral stands open, and light pours out of it. A player who never leaned on Pip never sees it. Every 10 Stress beyond 1,000 adds 1% to its HP.
- *The fight.* The screen is white glare, and the party are silhouettes; the being is the light itself, too bright to draw except at its edges. Pip's stock was always made of its stones, and it has taken them back, so items can't be used and Fuyori's Mix is the only source. Every 4th of its turns it Saves, making doubles of everyone as they stand. Three of its turns later it Loads, swapping everyone for those doubles: HP, KOs, statuses and the turn order on both sides go back to the Save. A double never copies Stress, because Stress is the soul, so Stress keeps climbing through every Load. The only damage that lasts is what lands in the 2 slots right after a Save, shown in the turn order as the moment being copied. A character who reaches 200 Stress doesn't flee here: they cough up a glowing stone, and their double takes their place under its control for the rest of the battle.
- *The puzzle.* Tempo. Sayo pulls hitters into the window, Ren pushes its turns out of it, Miren Jumps 5 slots early so he lands inside it, and Shio clears any Lost Turn that falls in it. The turns between a Save and a Load are free practice that only cost Stress.
- *Phases.* At 75% HP Lost Turns start falling inside the window. At 50% the window shrinks to 1 slot. At 25% each Load hands Stress back to whoever gave it to Pip, up to 30 per character per Load. At 10% it calls Kiri home: unless she acted in the last window, the next Load swaps her for nothing, and she is gone for the rest of the battle.
- *Reward.* The seam closes and Pip's Stress drops to 0. Pip sleeps for the first time. Pip's Comfort no longer costs Pip anything, and the party keeps one stone: once per battle, roll the turn order back to the start of the round.

**The One That Walks While You Sleep** (Disc 3). *Inspired by Slumber.*

- *What it is.* Something taller than the sky walks across Aurel every night, and only sleepers see it. Every sleeping person is one of its footsteps: when it walks, they get up and walk with it, and in the morning they wake remembering things their hands did. Waking up in each other's beds at inns was the smallest part of it. Awake eyes can't hold its shape. In battle it is two legs that disappear above the clouds, made of thousands of sleepwalkers moving in step.
- *Finding it.* Rest at every inn in Aurel in a row, with no battles in between. After the last one, the party wakes in nightclothes in an open field, mud to the knees, with no memory of the night. Natsu is missing. Her footprints lead to a town where every resident is standing asleep in the street, all facing the same way. To follow her, the party has to go back to sleep.
- *The fight.* Natsu walks in its step, so she can't be used and the party has no single-target healer. Only sleepers can reach it. Any character can use Dream (Delay 3) to fall asleep. Sleepers act on their own: the game picks their commands, their slots in the turn order show only a closed eye, and nothing they do is shown until they wake, when the log plays back what their hands did. Each sleeper's action has a 1 in 4 chance to hit an ally instead. Kiri's Lullaby puts the whole active party to sleep at once, at the usual Danger cost. Kugai can't hear lullabies and never sleeps: his new command Rouse wakes one ally.
- *Footsteps.* It steps every 4 slots. A step tramples every awake character for heavy damage and adds 5 Stress to every sleeper. Awake means control and getting crushed; asleep means safety and not knowing what you are doing.
- *Phases.* At 75% HP sleepers no longer wake when hit, only from Rouse. At 50% Natsu walks out in front of it, still asleep, and heals it on every step until Kugai rouses her; once awake she rejoins the party. At 25% anyone asleep when it steps has a 50% chance to stay asleep for the rest of the battle. At 10% it lies down: every character falls asleep at the start of each round, Kugai alone stays awake, and the fight ends blind, with the full log shown only after the last blow.
- *Reward.* Aurel stops walking in its sleep, and party members stop waking in each other's beds. Natsu's Cure stops adding Stress after 8 casts.

**Our Lady Under Aurel** (postgame, the Quiet). *Inspired by Madonna.*

- *What it is.* The thing the Crystals were pressed against is a mother. Beneath the world lies a woman larger than the continent, shaped exactly like the statue in every chapel of the Church of the Still Ear, which is where the statue's shape came from. She has been giving birth to Aurel over and over; every cycle is another birth, and every generation of heroes another set of her children. The four heroes of the last cycle were her children's hands, pushed up through the earth. Since the silence she has been weeping blood, and it rises in every well.
- *Finding it.* Only after beating the other three, the Ruri Garden and the Ear of the Ear. In the Quiet, every chapel statue in Aurel starts weeping blood. Follow the blood downhill. All of it runs to where the four Crystals stood.
- *The fight.* Four fronts at once, one at each of her hands where a Crystal stood, sharing one turn order. Before the fight the whole roster is split into four teams of three. Empty places are filled by the Grey Mother and, if Tetsugi was laid to rest, by his feathers, both AI-controlled. There is no Swap. Each front takes on its region's old wrongness:
  - **Wind (Ostrel):** a Lost Turn every 4 slots. Wants Shio or Kugai.
  - **Water (Vesk):** Undertow pushes the team back 3 slots every turn. Wants Sayo or an Inspired Rally.
  - **Fire (the Calm):** the footprints use each character's last command against them, so the team can never repeat itself. Wants Fuyori's Mix and a wide skill spread.
  - **Earth (Lumen):** the warm walls heal her 3% per turn unless all of them are hit at once. Wants Kuro's Darkga.
- *The rule over everything.* She has one HP pool, and damage only counts if all four fronts hit her within the same 4 slots; otherwise she forgives it and it heals. If a front has nobody standing for 2 of her turns, she gathers that team back into herself and the battle is lost.
- *Phases.* At 75% HP a KO on any front adds 15 Stress to everyone on every front. At 50% the four heroes remember they were her children, and each turn one team trades fronts with another, so every team has to survive any front's rule. At 25% anyone KO'd is reborn at once as one of her children on the next front over: an enemy with their face and skills. At 10% she sings the lullaby she has sung to every cycle, the turn order goes blank except during Kugai's turns, since he alone can't hear it, and the finishing blow is every character still standing using a Limit Break within one pass of the turn order, each in a different slot.
- *Reward.* She stops weeping and the wells run clear. The Quiet ending gains one last scene: Kugai, alone in the classroom, hears someone humming a lullaby.

## Endings

**Standard ending: The Quiet.** The world is saved and totally silent. Survivors rebuild Aurel using written signs and gestures. Years later, Kiri teaches children in a village school. A girl in the back row raises her hand and says she can hear something, very faintly. She hums it. Through the window, a new star is shining.

**Secret ending: The Fifth Hero.** Unlocked by completing Tetsugi's Feathers, recruiting both optional members and beating the First Save. In the last moment, Kiri takes the spiral shell and walks into the last turn of the Great Spiral with it. The world keeps its sound, and the hum never returns.

In the epilogue, four young adventurers meet in a sunny village and feel an instant sense of destiny. They do not notice the new Crystal at the edge of town, shaped like a woman holding a small shell to her ear.

**After the credits.** In a rebuilt café in Vesk, a waitress drops a tray. A beautiful customer helps her pick it up and smiles. Behind the counter, another customer with the same face is smiling too.
